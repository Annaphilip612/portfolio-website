01 / CASE STUDY

A daily routine worth redesigning

I didn't arrive at this project through a product trend. I arrived at it by watching my grandmother manage a routine she had to repeat, day after day. The meter, strips and lancing device each had their own small steps; together, they made something essential feel like a chore. I started wondering: what if the experience around checking glucose could feel simpler, clearer and less tiring?

That personal question became a wider design investigation. I looked beyond the meter itself — at preparing the kit, handling small components, getting a reading, recording it and returning to that information later. The opportunity was not just to redesign an object, but to make the whole everyday experience easier to manage.

How might we make everyday glucose monitoring simpler, more accessible and easier to manage?

03 / CASE STUDY

Empathy map & research synthesis

This is a synthesis of documented research themes and the initial observation. It is not a set of verbatim participant quotes.

THINKS & FEELS

Wants a dependable result; may feel burdened by a repeated multi-step routine.

SEES

A meter plus strips, lancets, storage needs and a separate record of readings.

DOES

Prepares equipment, handles small parts, performs the test, reads and records results.

PAINS

Fine-motor handling, repeated setup, consumable management and fragmented information.

GAINS

Fewer steps, more comfortable handling, clearer feedback and easier access to history.

DESIGN OPPORTUNITY

Treat the physical product and information system as one connected experience.

06 / CASE STUDY

Explore the sensing landscape

I investigated several alternative sensing directions to understand whether conventional finger-prick testing could eventually be reduced.

TECHNOLOGY EXPLORATION

Near-infrared spectroscopy (NIR)

Explored how near-infrared light interacts with biological tissue.

TECHNOLOGY EXPLORATION

Raman spectroscopy

Investigated molecular information obtained through light scattering.

TECHNOLOGY EXPLORATION

Bioimpedance

Explored whether electrical properties of tissue could contribute to measurement approaches.

TECHNOLOGY EXPLORATION

Mid-infrared spectroscopy

Studied another optical sensing direction and its potential relevance to glucose measurement.

These are research directions, not proven solutions. Non-invasive glucose measurement remains technically challenging and would require extensive engineering and clinical validation.

07 / CASE STUDY

Proposed design direction

GlucoSense explored a product-and-system approach rather than assuming that a new sensor alone would solve the experience.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Simplified physical interaction

Reconsider device preparation and component handling, especially for users with dexterity challenges.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Accessible everyday use

Reduce unnecessary effort and account for different user capabilities and levels of familiarity.

DESIGN PRINCIPLE

Information continuity

Explore a connected way to organise and revisit readings over time.

08 / CASE STUDY

What the project established

A research-led investigation into the physical and digital burden of everyday glucose monitoring.

  • Mapped the conventional testing routine and its repeated interactions.
  • Identified five opportunity areas: component complexity, handling, consumable management, repeated effort and reading management.
  • Reframed the brief around both product design and system design.
  • Explored non-invasive sensing technologies without claiming clinical feasibility.

Reflection: A medical product should not only perform its function. It should make the experience of living with that function easier.
